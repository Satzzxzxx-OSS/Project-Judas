## What was built

- A compact system: Terra (R 60 m, radial g 7) with a home base, trees, lakes and a
  comm tower; Ruby (R 42 m, g 5) with spires, boulders and the data-core outpost;
  and a 30-rock asteroid belt between them. Everything else is generated by
  `tools/build_system.py`.
- **Ship:** a dynamic rigid body (3000 kg), flown entirely by script.
  - Mouse, stick and roll set a target angular velocity. Thrusters apply forces.
  - Flight assist cancels drift and hovers against gravity, and auto-levels near the ground.
  - Boost, a speed cap, and a soft boundary that keeps the system inside the view range.
  - Collision damage, and the hull repairs itself after 4 s without damage.
  - Chase camera with rotational lag, plus a cockpit camera. Exhaust particles and
    engine sound scale with thrust.
- **Cannons:** twin alternating cannons, converging on whatever is under the reticle.
  Bolts are 48 pooled render-only entities moved by script, with raycast hits and an
  impulse at the hit point.
- **Raiders:** spawned prefabs. They orbit their target, lead their shots and avoid
  planets and asteroids. Destroyed raiders explode (particles and sound) and spawn
  5 physical debris prefabs.
- **On foot:** a CharacterMotor walking all the way around spherical planets
  (radial gravity, fast reorientation), with jump, run and footsteps.
  - Board within 8 m; exit only when landed and slow.
  - The exit spot is chosen by testing four sides of the ship for room with a capsule cast.
- **Mission:** board, wave 1, land on Ruby and grab the data core on foot, ambush
  wave, return home, then endless free flight.
  - Deaths respawn you at home, with spawn protection and a wave reset.
  - Pause and finish menus.
- **Presentation:**
  - World-space target markers, clamped to stay visible beyond the far plane.
  - A starfield made of geometry, following the camera.
  - A baked nebula environment and generated audio (cannons, explosions, hits,
    engine loop, footsteps, pickup).

## Engine / tooling findings

### S1. Quadratic per-step cost from entity count in scripted worlds (engine performance bug)
With **one empty script** in the world, plain render-only entities (no body, no
script) make every fixed step slower, roughly quadratically. Without any script
the cost is flat.

**Repro** (`notes/repro_entity_scaling/`): `make.py N scripted` writes a scene with N
render-only boxes and optionally one entity with `fixedUpdate(){}`. Then run
`JUDAS_TEST_SCRIPT=steps.txt judas repro.judasproj` with `steps.txt` = `STEPS 3000`:

| scripted | boxes | 3000 steps |
|---|---|---|
| no | 0 / 400 / 800 | 0.20 / 0.23 / 0.27 s |
| yes | 0 / 400 / 800 | 0.24 / **5.09** / **18.68** s |

The likely cause is `RuntimeWorld::ScriptObjects()` (`WorldScripts.cpp:18`). It copies
every entity's full `SceneObject` through a per-entry `RuntimeDefinition` lookup.
It is called several times per step: script `Synchronize` in update and fixed,
`GameSession.cpp:134`, and navigation. The engine profiler showed the growth in
"Fixed simulation step" exclusive time (1.23 → 3.99 ms) and in JS update/fixedUpdate
overhead (0.33 → 1.1 ms), not in game scripts.

**Impact here:** a 420-star starfield tripled step cost. **Workaround:** trimmed
decoration (100 stars, single-entity trees, larger pad tiles; 375 objects in total).
Scenes need to stay small.

### S2. Script camera far plane is fixed at 500 m (missing capability)
`WorldPresentation.cpp:462` builds the script-view projection with `0.1f, 500.f`.
JS has no near/far setting. A space game can't show anything beyond 500 m.
**Workarounds:**
- Compress the system: planets about 370 m apart, a soft boundary at 360 m from the belt.
- Put stars on a 470 m shell that follows the camera.
- Clamp target markers to 430 m along their direction, scaled to keep their angular size.

### S3. Syntax errors in imported modules are reported against the importing slot, without file or line (diagnostics gap)
A `const` redefinition inside `autopilot.js` (imported by `game.js`) was reported as:
`script asset=5ace…0001 entity=30 slot=1 module: SyntaxError: invalid redefinition of lexical identifier`.
Asset `…0001` is `game.js`; the message names neither `autopilot.js` nor a line number.
**Repro:** declare `const r` twice in one block of any module imported by a slot's
script, then Play.

### S4. No JS gravity query for arbitrary points or rigid bodies (missing capability)
Only CharacterMotor exposes sampled gravity (`state.gravity`). The ship and raiders
need gravity for hover assist and landing logic, so `shared.js` re-implements the
authored radial fields from generated scene data. They can drift apart if the scene
changes without regenerating. The constant-magnitude-inside-region model was read
from `RadicalGravity.h`.

### S5. The baked environment is too low-resolution for stars (limitation)
Environments bake to a maximum width of 512 (I used 256). Single-pixel stars show up
as large blurry squares in the background (seen in early captures).
**Workaround:** a starless nebula environment plus the geometry starfield (which runs into S1).

### S6. Script UI can't be positioned, so there are no screen-space target indicators (documented limitation)
There's no layout setter, so target brackets and lead indicators can't follow
targets on screen. **Workaround:** world-space glowing markers (see S2 for clamping).

### S7. CharacterMotors are invisible to physics queries (documented limitation)
Enemy bolts are raycasts, so they can't hit the on-foot pilot.
**Workaround:** a segment-to-capsule distance test against the pilot in `bolts.js`.

### S8. No per-instance data for `spawnPrefab` (minor gap)
There's no way to pass initial velocity or properties to a spawned instance.
Debris velocity goes through a module-level map that `debris.js` reads on its first
fixed step.

### S9. Fracture needs offline-cooked deformable assets (workflow limitation)
M63 fracture couldn't be used for exploding ships without an offline cook pipeline.
Scripted debris prefabs were used instead.

### S10. Tooling (same as the parkour experiment)
- The harness can't drive JS input, and its screenshots ignore `world.setView`.
- `JUDAS_TERRAIN_SCREENSHOT` takes one capture at 10 s and nothing under modal UI.
- **Process lesson:** I first tried desktop screenshots (`spectacle`) for more
  captures. Later captures grabbed whatever window was focused on your desktop
  instead of the game. I deleted those images unviewed beyond the first ones I
  opened, and switched to engine-only capture. An in-engine multi-shot capture
  option would avoid the need for desktop capture.
- The window title is still "Project Judas", not the project name.

### Things that worked well
- Radial gravity plus CharacterMotor: walking all the way around a 60 m planet, with
  correct up and reorientation, needed no game code beyond the walking controller.
- Writing `angularVelocity` directly plus `applyForce` gave responsive arcade flight
  on a real rigid body with real collisions.
- `spawnPrefab` hierarchies (body plus render children, scripts, spatial audio
  emitter) worked first time. Debris collided and settled on planets under radial gravity.
- `physics.raycast/sphereCast/capsuleCast` covered landing detection, exit-spot
  checks, cannon convergence, line of sight and test-pilot avoidance.
- Particle bursts take the emitter's current pose, so one teleported emitter serves
  every explosion. Unlit materials with per-slot colour overrides made glowing bolts and markers easy.

## My own game-code bugs (found and fixed)

1. Cannons converged at a fixed 180 m, so both bolts missed raiders at 45 m. They now
   converge on the range under the reticle.
2. Raiders led their shots with absolute velocity but their bolts inherit the raider's
   velocity. Switched to relative lead.
3. Flat-box landing pads on small spheres had rims 1.6–1.8 m high, so the pilot
   couldn't walk back onto them. Replaced with curvature-following tiles.
4. Raiders camped the respawn point and killed the pilot repeatedly. Added
   Ruby-only pilot hunting, spawn protection and a wave reset on death.
5. The hull bar used `|`, which was also my HUD field separator, so the bar was cut off.
6. Difficulty: raiders were initially far too lethal. Tuned damage, fire rate and
   spread, and added hull repair.
7. Test pilot: steering lag, no line-of-sight feed-forward, no obstacle avoidance
   (it crashed into an asteroid), and a landing approach that stalled 3 m off the pad.
8. A duplicate `const` identifier (which surfaced S3).

## Known game limitations

- No exiting the ship in space (EVA). A CharacterMotor in zero-g has no propulsion,
  so exiting is only allowed when landed.
- The planets are small (they have to fit within the 500 m view, S2). There's no
  atmosphere effect and no orbital mechanics; gravity is constant inside each region.
- Raider AI is simple orbit-and-shoot; raiders don't collide-avoid each other.
- Stars can draw in front of a planet whose far side is beyond 470 m (rare at this scale).
