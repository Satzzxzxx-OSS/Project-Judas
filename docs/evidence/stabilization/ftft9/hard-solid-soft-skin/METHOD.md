# Real-solid exclusion with preferred particle skin

This production approximation treats a particle as a liquid volume sample with a preferred collision skin, not as an undeformable finite sphere.

1. Try the legacy two preferred-skin passes first. A full proposed projection that ends strictly outside the real union and preserves every stationary box side may recover an overlapping prediction directly, retaining the original float arithmetic. Starting outside, its entire correction segment is tested as well. Fully feasible proposals keep legacy owner/normal velocity-row coalescing; exact primitive encounter data is separate geometric scratch. When a preferred proposal cannot establish a physical exterior, use the existing moving-solid swept/endpoint construction twice at radius zero as a lazy hard fallback. A hard surface point rounded inward may advance to its next representable coordinates along the known outward normal, at most 32 construction attempts; every attempt is counted. Radius-zero terrain projection stops only at nonnegative signed distance. Every actual displacement enters `solidCorrections`; it contributes no reconstructed kinetic impulse. Hard contact velocity normals are added only on this fallback path.
2. Validate the final point strictly against the represented, unexpanded union: box separation must be nonnegative, sphere squared distance must be at least squared radius, and radial-terrain signed distance must be nonnegative. No negative-distance error band is accepted.
3. If the hard passes leave an overlapping point or cross an encountered stationary real box, enumerate physical face/edge/corner intersections of encountered box primitives. Search their immediately adjacent representable coordinates; select the nearest strictly validated candidate. Validate against all endpoint geometry and all stationary box path barriers. The rare operation cannot use expanded particle-skin planes. If no verified candidate exists, increment `unresolvedGeometryCount` and throw an explicit error.
4. Preserve the existing two preferred-clearance passes, with blocked preferences retained across both. Apply each existing preferred-radius collision correction only along its segment up to the first real-solid entry. Box entry uses slab intersection and sphere entry a quadratic. Radial terrain retains the existing eight slices/eight bisections, with strict endpoint validation; this is not an exact continuous terrain-intersection guarantee.
5. When physical geometry clips a skin correction, preserve its achieved directional clearance during the rest of that particle projection call. A later soft proposal cannot undo the first blocked clearance. This deterministic ordering resolves incompatible skin preferences; physical exclusion takes priority.
6. Retain contact owner, normal, point and wall velocity when the skin yields. Existing velocity response owns the contact impulse. Particle mass and cavity coupling are unchanged.

Diagnostics expose rare hard repair count, candidate work, maximum hard repair distance, unresolved geometry, clipped skin proposals, prevented soft reversals and hard-surface rounding attempts. A strict failure is an observed unsupported geometry, not a successful approximation.

This is not conservative continuum moving-boundary remapping, exact finite-radius exclusion, or a new normal/contact law. Historical radius-expanded escape experiments remain rejected evidence. The first two hard-first candidates preserved the required closing-gap endpoint but changed an unchanged microrest witness (0.0519179 m mean motion versus the accepted 0.05 m limit). The second source is retained under `second-candidate-source/`. The current feasible-skin-first correction has not yet completed its parent-run acceptance gates; no tolerance or fixture is changed.
